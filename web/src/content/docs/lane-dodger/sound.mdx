There are no audio files in this project. The five effects are arithmetic,
generated when the game starts.

Recording or licensing five blips is faster than writing a synthesiser. It costs
a folder of binaries nobody can diff, a licence question on each one, and a
tuning loop that runs through an audio editor. Six hundred lines of arithmetic
gives sounds you change by changing a number.

## One oscillator with an envelope

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="Layer" />

A frequency sweep, an amplitude envelope, and an optional low pass to take the
fizz off noise. `delay` staggers a layer, which is how the arpeggios are built
with no sequencing code.

The coin is two layers, a triangle and a quieter sine an octave up:

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="coin" />

The crash is six. The last three are the falling triad that plays over the game
over screen:

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="crash" />

That is one sound, not two. Playing the thud on impact and the sting when the
retry prompt appears means the game has to remember whether it has played the
sting yet, for a run it has already lost. Layers take a delay, so the pause is
in the waveform and no state is needed.

## The renderer

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="render" />

The sweep is exponential, not linear. Pitch is logarithmic, so a linear sweep
sounds like it accelerates.

Layers are summed and soft clipped with `tanh` rather than clamped. Stacked
layers overshoot, and clamping shears the peaks flat, which is audible as
distortion.

## The envelope, and the click

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="envelope" />

The envelope reaches exactly zero at both ends of every layer.

A clip that begins at full amplitude begins with a step from silence to whatever
the waveform happened to be at. A step is a broadband impulse. It is a click,
and it is louder and more annoying than the effect it belongs to. The same
applies at the end. Four milliseconds of fade in is enough, and the decay lands
on zero rather than being cut off there.

## Testing a waveform

The synthesiser holds no raylib types. It fills a buffer with samples and stops.
So the sounds are checked without an audio device, which is just as well,
because CI has no speakers.

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="no sound begins or ends with a click" />

Then the mix. Absolute frequencies are taste and can move. The arrangement
cannot: the crash is the low long loud one, and the lane tick fires on every
input and must not shout over a pickup.

<GameSource file="src/audio/synth.zig" decl="the sounds sit in the right places against each other" />

Brightness there is zero crossings per second. It is a crude pitch estimate and
good enough to tell a chime from a thud.

Measured, the five come out as intended. The coin sits at about 1130 Hz over
85 ms. The crash runs 900 ms with 81 percent of its energy below 500 Hz. The
lane tick is shortest and quietest. `zig build sounds` writes them all out as
`.wav` for listening to.

## Optional at runtime

No sound card, no ALSA in the container, no click in the browser tab yet: all of
those leave the device unopened, and every call above turns into nothing.

<GameSource file="src/platform/audio.zig" decl="init" />

A game that refuses to start because it could not open a mixer is worse than a
silent one. The real binary is run with a deliberately broken ALSA configuration
to check it still exits cleanly.

Sound changed no rule in this game, because it reads the same
[event stream](/learn/lane-dodger/entities/) the particles read. The fifty-five
tests were still green afterwards, and none of them needed a speaker.
